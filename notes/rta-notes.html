Residential Tenancies Act: Study Notes

Alberta, Residential Tenancies Act, SA 2004, c. R-17.1, and its regulations
Disclaimer: These are summary study notes, not legal advice. Fees, limits and interest rates change. Check the current Act at kings-printer.alberta.ca and guidance from Service Alberta and the RTDRS before relying on any figure.

1. Scope and Key Concepts

  • Applies to: residential tenancy agreements and residential premises in Alberta. Mobile home sites are covered by the separate Mobile Home Sites Tenancies Act.
  • Does not apply to (s. 2), among others: rooms where the tenant shares a kitchen or bathroom with the owner who lives there, hotels and motels, nursing homes and care facilities, and emergency shelters.
  • Cannot contract out: any waiver of a tenant's rights under the Act is void, and agreement terms that conflict with the Act are unenforceable.
  • Tenancy types: periodic (weekly or monthly, which continue until either party gives proper notice) or fixed term (which ends automatically on the end date, so no notice is needed).
  • Where disputes go: the Residential Tenancy Dispute Resolution Service (RTDRS) or the courts (Alberta Court of Justice or Court of King's Bench).

2. The Tenancy Agreement

  • An agreement can be written, oral or implied. The Act applies to all of them.
  • If the agreement is written, the landlord must give the tenant a signed copy within 21 days. The tenant may withhold rent until they receive it.
  • The landlord must give the tenant a name and address in Alberta where notices can be served.
  • Terms the Act implies into every agreement are called covenants, and they cannot be removed by agreement.

4. Inspection Reports (s. 19)

  • Inspection reports are required within one week before or after the tenant moves in, and within one week before or after the tenant moves out.
  • The landlord and tenant inspect together. Both sign the report, and the tenant receives a copy.
  • If the tenant refuses or does not attend after reasonable opportunity, the landlord may do the inspection alone and note that on the report.
  • The reports are the main evidence in any deposit dispute, so photos with dates help.

5. Rent and Rent Increases

  • No rent cap: Alberta sets no maximum percentage, but it does set rules on frequency and notice.
  • Frequency (s. 14): a landlord may raise rent at most once every 365 days (counted from the start of the tenancy or the last increase).
  • Notice for a periodic tenancy: 3 full tenancy months for monthly tenancies, or 12 full tenancy weeks for weekly tenancies. The notice must be in writing.
  • Fixed term: the rent cannot be increased during the term. The landlord can change the rent only for a new agreement.
  • No unilateral rent withholding: the tenant must keep paying rent and apply for a remedy, such as an abatement (reduction), through the RTDRS or the court.

6. Rights and Obligations During the Tenancy

Landlord covenants (s. 16)

  • Make the premises available to the tenant on the agreed date.
  • Not interfere with the tenant's peaceful possession.
  • Keep the premises in a condition that meets the Minimum Housing and Health Standards (Public Health Act).

Tenant covenants (s. 21)

  • Pay rent on time.
  • Not significantly interfere with the rights of the landlord or other tenants.
  • Not do anything illegal on the property, and not endanger people or property.
  • Not do significant damage, and keep the premises reasonably clean.
  • Move out when the tenancy ends.

Landlord entry (s. 23)

  • The landlord needs 24 hours' written notice for entry between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. Entry is not allowed on a Sunday or the tenant's religious holiday.
  • The notice must state the reason and the date and time window, and it must be signed.
  • Permitted reasons are inspecting the premises, making repairs, pest control, and showing the unit to a prospective tenant or buyer.
  • No notice is needed in an emergency, with the tenant's consent at the time, or if the tenant has abandoned the premises.

7. Ending a Tenancy

A notice must be in writing, signed, and dated, and it must state the termination date. Landlord notices must also state the reason (s. 12).

8. Dispute Resolution

  • The RTDRS is a faster and cheaper alternative to court. Hearings are usually by phone, and the filing fee is about $75.
  • The RTDRS hears claims for possession, deposits, rent arrears, damages and abatement, up to the monetary limit of the Alberta Court of Justice ($100,000; check the current figure).
  • The applicant must serve the application on the other party before the hearing.
  • An RTDRS order is filed with the Court of King's Bench and enforced like a court order. An order can be appealed to the Court of King's Bench.
  • Limitation period: generally 2 years from when the claim was discovered (Limitations Act).
  • Service Alberta Consumer Investigations can investigate offences, such as illegal entry or failing to put a deposit in trust. Fines can apply.
